History Hologram started at a 48-hour Hack Club hackathon in Sydney.

The theme was:

*the crux of storytelling.*

Our team composition was slightly cursed.

One of us basically only knew hardware.

Another knew Godot and TypeScript.

I mostly knew Go, C and Python, with a bit of TypeScript.

We also had **no hardware tools whatsoever**.

Naturally, we ended up building the only hardware project at the hackathon.

The idea was to turn historical stories into little Pepper's Ghost holograms.

Instead of reading about something like ANZAC Cove through a wall of text, you could watch a 3D scene unfold with narration, camera movement, audio and models, then project the whole thing into a clear reflector sitting on an iPad.

## The hardest part was apparently buying plastic

Because we were doing a physical Pepper's Ghost display, we needed a clear sheet to build the reflector.

This somehow became a major engineering problem.

We spent **hours** deciding what plastic to buy.

Acrylic?

Polypropylene?

Clear film?

How thick?

Would it bend?

Would it reflect enough light?

Would it look terrible on an iPad?

Eventually we decided we actually had to leave the venue and buy something.

The organisers did not want to come with us.

At all.

They were comfortable.

We were the only group trying to leave.

And they basically weren't leaving unless they had to come with us.

So after an unreasonable amount of discussion, we dragged everyone into our hardware-acquisition side quest anyway.

Very efficient use of a 48-hour hackathon.

## The software became a tiny scene engine

We did not want to hardcode one ANZAC animation.

So the stories are written in YAML.

Something like:

```yaml
actors:
  - id: hero
    src: ../models/anzac-hero.glb
    position: [0, 0, 0]

moments:
  - at: 0
    label: ARRIVAL

  - at: 12
    label: FIRST LIGHT
```

Then the same timeline controls:

* camera movement
* models
* narration
* subtitles
* ambience
* effects

The React app does not know what ANZAC Cove is.

It just knows how to play a scene.

That was probably the most important design decision, because it meant the project became more than one hackathon demo.

You could make another historical story by adding assets and editing YAML instead of writing another frontend.

## Making the "hologram"

Pepper's Ghost is basically a reflection trick.

Put a clear angled surface over a bright screen and the reflected image appears to float in space.

For a four-sided pyramid, you render the same scene four ways around the centre:

So the pipeline became roughly:

```text
YAML story
    ↓
timeline
    ↓
React + Three.js
    ↓
3D scene
    ↓
four mirrored views
    ↓
iPad
    ↓
clear plastic pyramid
    ↓
"hologram"
```

We also kept a normal 3D mode because developing while constantly balancing a plastic pyramid on an iPad would have been miserable.

## The presentation went... okay

The actual hackathon presentation was not exactly flawless.

The hologram was hard to see.

The venue was too bright.

We had recorded narration, but it was not properly synced with the scene.

Some things worked better on our screen than through the reflector.

And after spending all that time building the physical part, the final effect was much less dramatic in the presentation than we wanted.

So when we finished presenting, I was not exactly thinking:

*yeah, we definitely won something.*

Then we somehow won the **GitHub award**.

I was genuinely surprised as hell.

Especially because our project was probably one of the least reliable things to demo in that room.

Everyone else could open a website.

We had:

```text
web app
+
3D engine
+
audio
+
iPad
+
plastic
+
room lighting
+
hope
```

But I think that was also what made it fun.

We were the only team trying to make the software physically leave the screen.
